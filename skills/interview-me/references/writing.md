# Interview: writing

Goal: capture the user's voice and reader-facing habits so drafts need fewer
rewrites. Samples beat descriptions — ask for them.

## Look first
Any writing already in the project or provided by the user (posts, emails,
docs). Infer nothing about voice from code.

## Topics (pick what matters; don't ask all)

- **Samples** — "Share something you wrote (or by someone) that sounds like you / how you want to sound, and something that doesn't." Start here.
- **Past annoyances** — "What do AI drafts do that you always delete?" (filler openers, "delve", hype, over-hedging…)
- **Reader** — who reads it, what do they already know, what do they do next?
- **Voice test** — write the same 3 sentences in three voices (plain/warm/formal) and ask which is closest. Then ask what's off about it.
- **Length & shape** — short and direct, or developed? Prose vs bullets vs headers? Answer first or build up?
- **Sentences** — short and punchy, or flowing? Contractions? First person?
- **Words** — banned words/phrases, jargon allowed or not, spelling variant (US/UK).
- **Punctuation & format** — em dashes, exclamation marks, emoji, bold, lists.
- **Hedging & confidence** — state claims plainly, or qualify?
- **Openers/closers** — greetings, sign-offs, summaries at the end: wanted or cut?
- **Language** — languages used, and does it vary by audience?

## Good entries look like
- **Lead with the point, then one supporting reason** — why: readers skim.
- **No hype words ("revolutionary", "seamless")** — why: sounds like marketing; I distrust it.
