# Interview: UI design

Goal: capture visual and interaction taste. People rarely describe design well
but react to it instantly — show options whenever you can.

## Look first
Existing styles, tokens, component library, screenshots, brand assets. If a
design system exists, record only where the user wants to deviate from it.

## Topics (pick what matters; don't ask all)

- **References** — "Name 2–3 products or sites whose look you love, and one you dislike. What specifically?" Start here.
- **Past annoyances** — "What do generated UIs do that you always change?" (default blue buttons, big gradients, cards everywhere…)
- **Audience & feel** — who uses it; 3 mood words; pairs: dense vs airy, playful vs serious, bold vs quiet.
- **A/B reactions** — if you can render, make two tiny mockups differing in one thing (density, radius, contrast) and ask which. Otherwise describe both in a line each.
- **Color** — palette or brand colors; light, dark, or both; how many accent colors.
- **Type** — font preferences; size and weight feel; headings loud or calm.
- **Spacing & shape** — tight vs generous; sharp vs rounded corners; borders vs shadows.
- **Motion** — none, subtle, or expressive?
- **Components** — library of choice (or none); icon style.
- **Responsive & accessibility** — mobile-first or desktop-first; contrast and keyboard minimums.
- **Interface copy** — tone of labels, errors, empty states.

## Good entries look like
- **Dense layouts, 4px grid, sharp corners** — why: it's a power-user tool.
- **No gradients or drop shadows; borders only** — why: flat looks calmer to me.
