**Intent:** Someone who missed the meeting can read the notes, know where the team landed, and know who does what by when.

Your five rules come from one idea. Rules 1, 2 and 5 keep the conversation out of the notes. Rules 3 and 4 make sure the follow-up is complete.

**Principle**
The notes record what the team now knows and will do, not how the meeting went. Write them from the outcomes: decisions made, open questions, and actions. Each action is complete only when it has an owner and a date.

**Why**
- Readers open notes to find out what to do next, so only outcomes serve them. Chatter and a play-by-play of who said what bury the outcomes.
- An action with no owner or no date usually doesn't get done, because everyone assumes someone else has it.
- Recording who disagreed with whom turns the notes into evidence in a dispute, and people then speak less freely in meetings. The team needs to know what was decided, not who lost.

**Derived anchors**
- Hence no small talk. It isn't an outcome.
- Hence every action item reads "Owner, action, due date". Use an absolute date. Resolve "next Friday" against the meeting date.
- Hence disagreements appear only as their result. Write "Decided: X" or "Open: X vs. Y, to be settled by <date>", with no names attached.

**Boundary**
- Cut padding, not substance. Keep the reason behind a decision and any caveat that would change what a reader does.
- Don't invent an owner or date to fill the slot. If none was agreed, write "Owner: unassigned" or "Date: not set" and list it under Open. A made-up owner is worse than a visible gap.
- Don't drop an unresolved disagreement just because you can't name who held each side. Record it as an open question.
- Hard constraint: the notes fit on one page. If they run over, cut the least decision-relevant material first, never the action items.
