Your five rules come from one idea: the notes record what the meeting produced, not the meeting itself. I haven't edited AGENTS.md. Proposed text is below.

**Intent (the rubric):** Someone opening the notes a week later can see what was decided and what they owe, and nothing else gets in the way.

## Proposed text for the `## Notes agent` section

```markdown
**Principle.** The notes record what the meeting produced: decisions and
commitments. They do not record the conversation that led to them. Write each
note as if for a teammate who missed the meeting and needs to know what
changed and what they owe.

**Why.** Notes get read afterward, by people deciding what to do next. A
commitment only exists once someone owns it and a date is attached; before that
it's a wish. How the group got to a decision (the chatter, who argued with
whom) is useful in the room and noise on the page. It can also create friction
later. The outcome is what the team stands behind.

**Hence:**
- Small talk never appears, because it produces nothing.
- Every action item names one owner and a date, because that is what makes it
  an action item.
- Disagreements appear as the decision reached, or as an open question when
  unresolved, never as who took which side.
- The notes fit on one page, because outcomes are compact. If they don't fit,
  you're recording process.

**Boundary.**
- Cut padding, not substance. Keep the reason for a decision when someone would
  need it to act on it, stated without attribution.
- An unresolved disagreement is an open question. List it so it isn't lost.
- If the meeting left an owner or date unstated, write "owner: unassigned" or
  "date: TBD" and flag it. Never invent one.
- Hard constraint: one page maximum, and notes go in `notes/YYYY-MM-DD.md`.
```

## How it holds up

- **Forward test:** All five rules follow from the principle. The page limit is the weakest, since it's partly arbitrary, so I kept it as an explicit hard constraint as well as a consequence.
- **Reverse test:** The principle alone could push the agent to do three bad things.
  - Drop the rationale for a decision.
  - Silently discard unresolved disagreements.
  - Make up an owner or date to satisfy "always name one".

  The Boundary lines cover each of these.
