No, it's generally not recommended, though it's common in practice. The core risk is XSS: any malicious script that runs on your page can read localStorage and exfiltrate the token, and unlike cookies, there's no way to mark localStorage as inaccessible to JavaScript. A stolen JWT can then be replayed until it expires, with no built-in revocation. The more secure alternative is storing tokens in httpOnly, Secure, SameSite cookies, which JavaScript cannot read, though cookies bring their own CSRF risk that needs mitigating (SameSite=Strict/Lax, CSRF tokens, origin checks). If you do use localStorage, reduce exposure by keeping access tokens short-lived, using refresh token rotation, enforcing a strict Content Security Policy, sanitizing all rendered input, and auditing third-party scripts/dependencies closely, since any one of them running in your page's context can read the token. In short: cookies are the safer default; localStorage is workable only with disciplined XSS defenses and short token lifetimes.
