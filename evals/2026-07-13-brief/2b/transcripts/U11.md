`git merge` and `git rebase` both integrate changes from one branch into another, but differ in history:

- **merge** adds a new commit that joins the two histories, preserving both branches' commits exactly as they happened. Non-destructive, but leaves a merge commit and can make history messier.
- **rebase** replays your commits on top of the target branch, creating new commits with new hashes. Result is a linear, cleaner history — but it rewrites history, so avoid rebasing commits already pushed/shared with others.

Rule of thumb: rebase local/private branches to keep history clean; merge when integrating shared/public branches.
