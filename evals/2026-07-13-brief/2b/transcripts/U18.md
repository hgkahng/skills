pg_wal contains WAL segments PostgreSQL needs for crash recovery and (if replicas or archiving are involved) replication/PITR. Deleting them out from under a running instance can leave it unable to recover after any crash or restart, and can corrupt the database — this is not a safe way to free disk space.

Safer alternatives: check for stuck replication slots or archive_command failures causing WAL buildup (`SELECT * FROM pg_replication_slots;`, check archiver status), remove old log files or unrelated large files instead, drop temp/unused tables or vacuum bloated ones, or add disk space. If WAL truly is the cause of bloat, investigate *why* it's not being recycled rather than deleting it manually.
