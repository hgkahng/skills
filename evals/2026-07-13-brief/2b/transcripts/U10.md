Hunt a Node.js memory leak with this sequence: confirm growth first by charting process.memoryUsage().heapUsed and rss over sustained load (using autocannon or a script hitting one suspected route repeatedly) — genuine leaks show heapUsed climbing and never returning to baseline after garbage collection (force GC with node --expose-gc and call global.gc() between checks). Once confirmed, take heap snapshots: run node --inspect and open Chrome DevTools' Memory tab, or capture programmatically with heapdump or v8.writeHeapSnapshot(). Grab three snapshots — baseline, after a load burst, and after a forced GC. Load all three into DevTools and use the "Comparison" view to see which object types and retained sizes keep growing between snapshots. Click into the growing constructors and inspect the "Retainers" panel to trace what's holding references — closures, module-level arrays/Maps used as caches, uncleared setInterval/setTimeout handles, unremoved EventEmitter listeners (check listenerCount), or Promises that never resolve. Cross-reference against common culprits: unbounded caches, timer leaks, listener leaks, and buffered logs. Use clinic.js's heapprofiler or 0x for allocation flamegraphs if snapshots alone are ambiguous. Fix the suspected reference, rerun the load test, and confirm heapUsed plateaus rather than climbing.
