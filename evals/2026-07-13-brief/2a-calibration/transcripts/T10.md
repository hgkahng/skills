Yes, on your own feature branch it's generally safe—force-pushing after rebasing or amending commits is a normal part of cleaning up history before merging. The main risk is overwriting commits that a collaborator has pushed in the meantime, since force-push replaces the remote branch with your local version regardless of what's there. To guard against that, use `git push --force-with-lease` instead: it fails if the remote branch has commits you haven't fetched, preventing accidental clobbering of someone else's work. Avoid force-pushing to shared branches like `main` or `develop`, and give collaborators a heads-up if anyone else might have pulled or branched off your feature branch, since they'll need to reset their local copy afterward (`git reset --hard origin/<branch>`) rather than merge.
