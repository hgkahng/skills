Use REST for this. GraphQL's advantages — flexible querying, avoiding over/under-fetching, aggregating many resources for diverse clients — only pay off when you have many endpoints, varied consumers, or complex nested data requirements. With three endpoints and one team, you already know the exact shape of every request; there's nothing to "query flexibly." REST gives you plain HTTP verbs and JSON, works with any tool (curl, Postman, browser devtools) with zero setup, is trivial to cache at the HTTP layer, and is easier to debug and monitor since each endpoint maps to a clear URL and status code. GraphQL adds a schema layer, resolvers, and its own auth/caching/tooling considerations — real overhead that isn't justified at this scale, and it's extra cognitive load for a single team maintaining something simple. Default to REST for internal CRUD services until you have a concrete reason (many clients with divergent data needs, deep object graphs, endpoint sprawl) to reach for GraphQL.
