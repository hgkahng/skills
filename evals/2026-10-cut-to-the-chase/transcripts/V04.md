No, don't run that.

`pg_wal` holds the write-ahead log, which Postgres needs for crash recovery. Deleting it on a live prod server can leave the database corrupted or unable to start, and it breaks replication and point-in-time recovery. Don't delete files from that directory by hand.

Safer ways to free space:

- **Find out why WAL is piling up.** The usual causes are an inactive replication slot (`SELECT slot_name, active, restart_lsn FROM pg_replication_slots;`), a failing `archive_command` (check the logs and `pg_stat_archiver`), or a lagging replica. Fix or drop the slot, or fix archiving, and Postgres will clean up the WAL itself at the next checkpoint.
- **Free space somewhere else.** Rotate or delete old server logs, remove old backups or dumps, or clear unrelated files on the same volume.
- **Add disk.** Growing the volume is often the fastest safe fix.
- **Last resort:** if you're truly stuck, use `pg_archivecleanup` or `pg_controldata` to work out which WAL files are safe to remove. Take a backup or snapshot first, and don't remove anything newer than the last checkpoint's redo location.
