A p-value is the probability of seeing data at least as extreme as yours if there were actually no effect (the "null hypothesis" is true).

A small p-value means your result would be unusual in a world with no effect, which counts as evidence against that no-effect assumption. By convention, p < 0.05 is often called "statistically significant."

Example: you flip a coin 10 times and get 9 heads. The p-value is the chance of getting a result that lopsided (9 or more heads, or 9 or more tails) from a fair coin, about 0.02. That is rare enough to doubt the coin is fair.

What it is not:
- It is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true.
- It does not measure how large or important the effect is.
