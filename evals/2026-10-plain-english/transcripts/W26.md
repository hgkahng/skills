**Why decouple:** With Adam plus L2, the penalty term λθ is added to the gradient, so it passes through Adam's adaptive scaling. The update becomes roughly η·(g + λθ)/(√v̂ + ε). Parameters with a large gradient history (large √v̂) get their decay shrunk, and parameters with small gradient history get decayed more. The effective regularization strength then depends on the gradient statistics rather than on λ alone, and it no longer matches what L2 is meant to do.

AdamW applies the decay directly to the weights, outside the adaptive step:

θ ← θ − η·m̂/(√v̂ + ε) − η·λ·θ

Every weight shrinks by the same relative factor each step, whatever its gradient history. With plain SGD, L2 and weight decay are equivalent. With adaptive methods they are not, and AdamW restores the intended behavior (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019).

**When it matters:**
- **Hyperparameter tuning.** In AdamW, the best λ is much less tied to the learning rate. With Adam plus L2, learning rate and λ interact, so you have to search them jointly.
- **Generalization.** AdamW usually generalizes better, especially in transformers and other large models trained with a schedule. The gap is largest when the regularization is doing real work.
- **Little difference:** with no or very small weight decay, or in short training runs, the two behave about the same.

In practice, use AdamW whenever you want weight decay with Adam. Typically you also exempt biases and normalization parameters from decay.
