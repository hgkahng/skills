Your model is memorizing the training data instead of learning patterns that carry over to new data. This is called overfitting.

A model with enough capacity can learn the exact examples it sees, including their noise and quirks. It scores nearly perfectly on those examples. Then it meets new data, where those quirks don't apply, and the score drops.

A gap of 99% versus 70% is large. These are the most common causes:

1. **The model is too big for the data.** Too many parameters and too few examples lets it memorize.
2. **You trained too long.** Past a certain point, training accuracy keeps rising while validation accuracy stalls or falls.
3. **The dataset is small or noisy.** There aren't enough examples to show the real pattern.
4. **Not enough regularization.** Regularization is anything that makes memorizing harder, such as dropout, weight decay, or a simpler model.

There are also causes that aren't overfitting, and they're worth checking:

- **The two sets differ.** If validation data comes from a different time, source, or group than training data, the model can fail even when it learned well.
- **A bug in the split.** Check that the split is random where it should be, and that classes are balanced the same way in both.
- **Leaked information.** If you scaled or selected features using all the data before splitting, the numbers can mislead you. Fit those steps on training data only.

What to try, in rough order:

- Plot training and validation accuracy by epoch. If validation peaks early and then falls, stop earlier (early stopping).
- Add more training data, or use data augmentation if it fits your problem.
- Use a smaller or simpler model.
- Add dropout, weight decay, or other regularization.
- Use cross-validation to check that 70% isn't just a bad split.
