Your model is overfitting. It has learned the training examples themselves, including their noise and quirks, instead of the general pattern. So it does well on data it has seen and poorly on data it hasn't.

The usual fixes, roughly in order of how often they help:

1. **Get more training data**, or add variety to what you have (for images, flips and crops; for text, paraphrases).
2. **Make the model simpler.** Fewer layers, fewer parameters, or a shallower tree.
3. **Add regularization.** Dropout, weight decay, or an L1/L2 penalty all discourage the model from fitting noise.
4. **Stop training earlier.** Watch validation loss and stop when it starts rising while training loss keeps falling.

One thing to check first: make sure no validation rows leak into training, and that both sets come from the same kind of data. If the split is unrepresentative, you can see a gap like this even without overfitting.
