Adam rescales each parameter's update by a running estimate of its gradient size. If you add weight decay as an L2 term in the loss, the decay goes into the gradient and gets rescaled too. So parameters with large gradient history are decayed less, and parameters with small gradient history are decayed more. The decay strength ends up depending on the gradient statistics, not on your weight decay setting alone. That is not what weight decay is supposed to do.

AdamW fixes this by leaving the decay out of the gradient. It shrinks the weights directly in a separate step: w ← w − lr · λ · w. Every weight shrinks by the same fraction, and Adam's rescaling never touches it.

When it matters:

- **Tuning.** In AdamW, the best learning rate and the best weight decay are mostly independent, so you can search them separately. With Adam plus L2, they are tangled together.
- **Generalization.** AdamW usually generalizes better than Adam plus L2. This shows up most in transformers and other large models trained with a learning rate schedule.
- **Little difference.** With plain SGD, the two forms are equivalent up to a rescaling of λ. With small weight decay, or short training, you may see no gap.

The source is Loshchilov and Hutter, "Decoupled Weight Decay Regularization" (2019).
