Treating this as a quick sanity check, so I'll stick to the two biggest problems. Both are fatal as written, and both are cheap to fix.

## Fatal: you're selecting on the test set

"Best test accuracy across 20 configs" means you pick the winner using test data and then report that same number. Even if the optimizer is no better than AdamW, the max of 20 noisy draws is biased upward. On CIFAR-10, run-to-run noise is often a few tenths of a percent, which is about the size of the gains optimizer papers usually claim. A reviewer will read this as test-set leakage plus "reporting max instead of mean", and I think they'd be right.

**Fix:** pick the config on a held-out validation split (for example 45k/5k from the training set), then report test accuracy once for that chosen config. Report mean and std over at least 3-5 seeds of the chosen config, not the best seed.

## Fatal: the baseline got no tuning and yours got 20 tries

AdamW at default settings against a method that got a 20-config sweep is a tuning asymmetry. The default learning rate and weight decay are rarely optimal for CIFAR-10 with a given architecture and schedule, so you may just be measuring "tuned vs. untuned". It's also the most likely way for the result to look good and then fail to replicate.

**Fix:** give AdamW the same budget. Use 20 configs sampled from a comparable search space, such as learning rate, weight decay, and warmup/schedule. Select on validation the same way for both. If your optimizer has more tunable knobs than AdamW, say so and match on number of trials, not number of knobs.

## Worth checking: does the claim match the metric?

"Generalizes better" is a different claim from "reaches higher test accuracy." To support it you'd want to show something like the train/test gap, or results on a shifted set such as CIFAR-10-C or CIFAR-10.1. If you only want to claim "better accuracy at equal tuning budget", that's fine, but word it that way.

Two smaller points:
- Compare at matched epochs and wall-clock, in case your optimizer costs more per step.
- A single architecture on CIFAR-10 is a thin base for a general claim. One more setting, such as a different model or dataset, would help a lot.

## Minimal version that fits a small budget

If compute is tight, here is the smallest design that would hold up:
1. Train/val/test split, with config selection on val only.
2. Equal tuning budget for both optimizers (say 20 trials each, or fewer if needed).
3. Final config for each, 3-5 seeds, test accuracy as mean ± std, evaluated once.

If the gap between the two means is smaller than the seed std, treat it as no difference. That's useful to learn early. A pilot at reduced epochs would tell you before you commit to the full runs.

A few questions that would change my advice:
- Is the 20-config sweep over your optimizer's hyperparameters only, or over shared ones like learning rate too?
- Which architecture and schedule are you using? A cosine or one-cycle schedule changes how much default AdamW leaves on the table.
- Do you have a validation split already, or is the plan to use the standard 50k/10k train/test?
