## Fatal

**1. Asymmetric tuning.** You swept 6 learning rates for your method and ran standard LoRA at its default. This alone could account for the whole 4 points. LoRA is known to be sensitive to learning rate, and the default is rarely optimal for a given model and task. A reviewer might argue that your result shows "tuned method beats untuned baseline" and says nothing about the variant. The fix is to give standard LoRA the same sweep (the same 6 LRs, or a grid centered on its own optimum) and compare best against best.

Related question: what did you select the best LR on? If it was GSM8K test accuracy, you have a second problem. Picking the best of 6 on the test set is leakage and inflates your number. Hold out a slice of the train set (e.g. 500 examples) as a dev set, select on that, and report test once.

**2. Single seed, with a gap near the noise floor.** GSM8K test has 1,319 problems. At about 60% accuracy, the binomial standard error on each number is roughly 1.3 points. That puts the SE of the difference near 1.9 points if the two models were independent. Your 4-point gap is therefore about 2 SE from test-set sampling alone. Training-seed variance (init, data order, dropout) comes on top. For fine-tuned 7B models on GSM8K, run-to-run spread of 1-2 points is common, but measure your own. Best-of-6 selection on your side and one draw on the baseline's side also bias the comparison toward your method. With one seed each, you can't distinguish a real effect from noise.

## Serious

**3. Parameter and compute matching.** Does your variant have the same number of trainable parameters and the same FLOPs per step as standard LoRA at the same rank? If it adds parameters (extra scaling vectors, a higher effective rank, a second matrix), compare against standard LoRA at matched parameter count too. A rank-sweep for the baseline (e.g. r = 8, 16, 32, 64) is the cleanest control. Otherwise, "better" may just mean "bigger".

**4. Evaluation protocol.** State and fix these for both methods: zero-shot or few-shot prompting, greedy or sampled decoding, answer extraction (strict match on `####` or flexible regex), and max generation length. Differences in extraction can swing GSM8K scores by several points. Use the same harness and the same script for both.

**5. One benchmark.** GSM8K is a narrow, math-only, well-saturated target. It's also likely present in some form in the base model's pretraining data. A single benchmark makes "my LoRA variant is better" a very thin claim. At minimum, add one more reasoning dataset (e.g. MATH subset) and one non-math task, so the claim isn't "better at GSM8K".

**6. No ablation.** If the variant has more than one new component, there's no evidence which one matters. Minimal table: full method, minus each component individually, and standard LoRA as the lower bound. If the components interact, say so and add the pairwise cells.

## Minor

- A 7B base model only. One more size (e.g. 1-3B for cheap replication) helps show the effect isn't specific to one model.
- Report mean ± std (or a bootstrap CI) over seeds, not the max.
- Log training loss curves. If your method simply converges faster, that's a different story from reaching a higher plateau, and the sweep over LRs may be hiding it.

## Budget: what's feasible on 4 A100s in two weeks

Fine-tuning a 7B with LoRA on GSM8K's 7.5k training examples is cheap. Time one run first, but I'd expect each to be on the order of an hour or less. That makes the full fix affordable, so no cut-down version is needed:

1. Sweep the same LR grid for both methods, selecting on a held-out dev split (1 seed each, ~12 runs).
2. Take the best LR per method and run 5 seeds each (10 runs), plus a rank-matched baseline at 3 seeds.
3. Evaluate on test once, with one harness. Report mean ± std and a paired comparison (e.g. a paired bootstrap over problems, or McNemar's test, since both models see the same 1,319 items).
4. Run the 2-3 ablations that matter, 3 seeds each.
5. If time remains, add a second benchmark.

Run the 4-GPU job as four parallel single-GPU runs, not data parallel, since seeds are embarrassingly parallel.

## Should you reconsider the direction?

Not on this evidence. A 4-point gap under asymmetric tuning is not a negative signal. It's an uninformative one. The cheap pilot that tells you whether to keep going is step 1 plus 2-3 seeds of each at the best LR. Here's what I'd expect and what would change my mind:

- If the gap shrinks to under about 1 point once the baseline is tuned, the contribution is mostly tuning, and I'd reconsider the paper's framing (maybe it's about robustness to LR, which is itself a testable claim).
- If the gap holds at 2+ points with non-overlapping seed ranges, you have a real result worth a paper, subject to the ablation and a second benchmark.
