I read this as a figure for a paper, so I used thin lines, `\footnotesize` labels and no decoration. The two curves differ by line style (solid for training, dashed for validation), so the figure still reads in black-and-white print. The annotation is the only colored element, in red, because it is the highlighted part.

The data is invented but follows the usual shape. Training loss decays smoothly from 1.20 to 0.04. Validation loss falls to its minimum of 0.43 at epoch 23 and then climbs to 0.65, with a few small bumps. That minimum is the "overfitting begins" point. The label sits in the empty band between the two curves, with a short arrow to a marker on the minimum, so it never touches a line.

Notes:
- It uses only packages in base TeX Live (`pgfplots` and `pgfplotstable`, plus the `arrows.meta` TikZ library).
- The data lives in one table, so the two curves cannot get out of sync. To use your real logs, replace that table with your own epoch, train and val columns. The marker and arrow coordinates `(23,0.43)` are hard-coded, so move them to your actual validation minimum.
- The width is `0.9\columnwidth`, so the plot scales to a single column in a two-column template. Outside a paper class, `\columnwidth` is the standalone page width.
- If you want a lecture-slide version, I would raise the line widths to about 1.5pt, increase the font size, and drop the dashed style in favor of color.
